US citizenship test practice: the 128 civics questions (2025 test)

Free practice for the civics part of the naturalization interview. Applications filed on or after 20 October 2025 take the 2025 civics test.

Which test do you take? It depends on the date you filed your application (Form N-400). Filed on or after 20 October 2025: the 2025 civics test (128 questions). Filed before 20 October 2025: the 2008 civics test (100 questions). Source: USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 12, Part E, Chapter 2.

How the 2025 civics test works

The officer asks you up to 20 of the 128 questions out loud. You pass with 12 correct answers. The officer stops once you have 12 correct, or once you have 9 wrong. If, when you file, you are 65 or older and have lived in the United States for periods totaling at least 20 years as a lawful permanent resident, you may study just the 20 questions marked 65/20: the officer asks 10 of them, you need at least 6 correct, and you may take the test in the language of your choice with an interpreter.

Questions about your own state (your senators, your representative, your governor, your capital) have answers that depend on where you live, and the names of the President, Vice President, Speaker and Chief Justice can change. USCIS keeps the current names at uscis.gov/citizenship/testupdates; the names it lists are shown under those questions with the date they were read. The practice interviews ask these questions too.
