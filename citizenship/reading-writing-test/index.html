The English reading and writing test: what counts as a pass

Last checked 12 November 2026.

The English part of the naturalization test has three pieces: speaking, reading and writing. Here is how the officer judges each one, from the USCIS Policy Manual.

The standard: ordinary English

You need to read, write, speak and understand English "in ordinary usage". That means simple words and grammar that can be understood, even with noticeable mistakes. You may ask the officer to repeat or rephrase a question.

Speaking

The officer judges your speaking from your answers to the usual interview questions, including the questions about your application. You pass if you generally understand and give meaningful answers.

Reading: one sentence out of three

The officer gives you up to three sentences. You need to read one correctly, and the officer stops as soon as you do.

  • Passes: you read the sentence without long pauses, so its meaning is clear. You may leave out short words or make pronunciation mistakes that do not hide the meaning.
  • Fails: you leave out or change an important word, pause for long stretches, or mispronounce words so badly that the meaning is lost, on all three sentences.

Writing: one sentence out of three

The officer reads out up to three sentences, and you need to write one in a way that can be understood. Do not use abbreviations unless the officer has approved them.

  • Passes: some grammar, spelling or capital-letter mistakes; leaving out short words that do not change the meaning; numbers written as words or as digits. Spelling, capitals or punctuation mistakes alone do not make you fail.
  • Fails: writing a different sentence, an abbreviation the officer has not approved, nothing, only one or two separate words, a sentence nobody can read, or mistakes that make the meaning unclear, on all three sentences.

If you do not pass

You are tested again on the failed part 60 to 90 days later. See What happens if you fail the citizenship test?
