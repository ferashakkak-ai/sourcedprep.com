The 65/20 rule for the civics test, explained

Last checked 12 November 2026.

Some older applicants take a much shorter civics test. USCIS calls this "special consideration", and its October 2025 policy alert labels it 65/20.

Who qualifies

You qualify if both of these are true on the day you file your Form N-400:

  • you are 65 or older, and
  • you have lived in the United States as a lawful permanent resident for periods that add up to at least 20 years.

(USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 12, Part E, Chapter 2.)

What changes

  • You study a short list of 20 specially marked questions instead of the full list. On the 2025 test the full list is 128 questions; on the 2008 test it is 100.
  • You are asked 10 of the 20 questions and need 6 correct.
  • You may take the civics test in the language of your choice, using an interpreter (USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 12, Part E, Chapter 2, exceptions table).

You still use the test version that matches your filing date: the 2025 list if you filed on or after 20 October 2025, the 2008 list if you filed before. (USCIS test updates page; Policy Manual.)

What about English?

People 50 or older with 20 years as a permanent resident, and people 55 or older with 15 years, are exempt from the English part of the test. If you qualify for 65/20, you also meet the 50/20 rule. The 50/20 and 55/15 groups still take the full civics test, in their own language if they wish. (Policy Manual, exceptions.)

Practice the 20 questions

The 2025 test page marks the 65/20 questions and has a 10-question 65/20 practice. The 2008 test page marks them for the older test. En español.
