What happens if you fail the citizenship test?

Last checked 12 November 2026.

Failing one part of the naturalization test at your interview does not end your application. An applicant has two chances to pass: the first examination and one re-examination. (USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 12, Part E, Chapter 2.)

You are tested again on the failed part only

The test has an English part (reading, writing, speaking and understanding English) and a civics part. If you fail any part, USCIS reschedules you for a re-examination between 60 and 90 days later. At the re-examination you are tested only on the part or parts you failed, and the officer must use different test forms from the first time.

You get your results the same day

The officer records your English and civics results and gives them to you at the end of the interview on Form N-652, unless a denial notice is given then.

A second failure means denial

If you fail the same part again at the re-examination, the application is denied for not meeting the educational requirements. Refusing to take a test, or not answering, counts as a failure. Missing the re-examination appointment, unless you are excused, also counts as a failed attempt.

If you appeal a denial

If your application is denied and you ask for a hearing, the officer gives you any part of the tests you failed, and you have one chance to pass it at the hearing.

Using the 60 to 90 days well

  • Read your Form N-652 to see which part you failed.
  • For civics, practice the list for your filing date: 2025 test or 2008 test.
  • Try a practice interview that stops the way the officer does (12 correct or 9 wrong on the 2025 test).
