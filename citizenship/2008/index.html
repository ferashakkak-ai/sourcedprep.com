US citizenship test practice: the 100 civics questions (2008 test)

For applications filed before 20 October 2025, which still take the 2008 civics test.

Which test do you take? It depends on the date you filed your application (Form N-400). Filed on or after 20 October 2025: the 2025 civics test (128 questions). Filed before 20 October 2025: the 2008 civics test (100 questions). Source: USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 12, Part E, Chapter 2.

How the 2008 civics test works

The officer asks up to 10 of the 100 questions out loud. You pass with 6 correct answers. The officer stops once you have 6 correct, or once you have 5 wrong. If, when you file, you are 65 or older and have lived in the United States for periods totaling at least 20 years as a lawful permanent resident, you may study just the 20 questions marked 65/20: you are asked 10 of them and need at least 6 correct.

Questions about your own state have answers that depend on where you live, and the names of officials can change. USCIS keeps the current names at uscis.gov/citizenship/testupdates; the names it lists are shown under those questions with the date they were read. The practice interviews ask these questions too.

American Government: Principles of American Democracy

  1. What is the supreme law of the land?
    • the Constitution
  2. What does the Constitution do?
    • sets up the government
    • defines the government
    • protects basic rights of Americans
  3. The idea of self-government is in the first three words of the Constitution. What are these words?
    • We the People
  4. What is an amendment?
    • a change (to the Constitution)
    • an addition (to the Constitution)
  5. What do we call the first ten amendments to the Constitution?
    • the Bill of Rights
  6. What is one right or freedom from the First Amendment?65/20
    • speech
    • religion

    • press
    • petition the government
  7. How many amendments does the Constitution have?
    • twenty-seven (27)
  8. What did the Declaration of Independence do?
    • announced our independence (from Great Britain)
    • declared our independence (from Great Britain)
    • said that the United States is free (from Great Britain)
  9. What are two rights in the Declaration of Independence?
    • life
    • liberty
    • pursuit of happiness
  10. What is freedom of religion?
    • You can practice any religion, or not practice a religion.
  11. What is the economic system in the United States?65/20
    • capitalist economy
    • market economy
  12. What is the “rule of law”?
    • Everyone must follow the law.
    • Leaders must obey the law.
    • Government must obey the law.
    • No one is above the law.

American Government: Rights and Responsibilities

  1. There are four amendments to the Constitution about who can vote. Describe one of them.

    • You don’t have to pay (a poll tax) to vote.
    • Any citizen can vote. (Women and men can vote.)
    • A male citizen of any race (can vote).

    • vote in a federal election

    • serve (do important work for) the nation (if needed)
    • be loyal to the United States
  7. How old do citizens have to be to vote for President?65/20

  8. What are two ways that Americans can participate in their democracy?
