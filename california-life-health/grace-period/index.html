Grace period and lapse notices for California life insurance

Last checked 13 November 2026.

California gives life insurance owners time to pay after a missed premium, and it adds notice rules so a policy does not lapse without warning. Here are the rules from the Insurance Code.

The 60-day grace period

  • Every life insurance policy issued or delivered in California must have a grace period of at least 60 days from the premium due date. (Cal. Ins. Code 10113.71(a))
  • The 60 days do not overlap any period of coverage that is already paid for. (10113.71(a))
  • The policy stays in force during the grace period. (10113.71(a))
  • For this rule, "life insurance policy" includes individual and group life policies, unless the code says otherwise. (10113.71(c))

The right to name a designee

Before an individual life policy can be issued or delivered in California, the applicant must be given the right to name at least one other person to receive notice if the policy is about to lapse or end for nonpayment. The insurer gives the applicant a form for this, with space for that person's name, address and phone number. (10113.72(a)) Each year the insurer must remind the owner that the designation can be changed or added to. The owner may change the designation more often. (10113.72(b))

Notice before lapse

  • An individual life policy cannot lapse or end for nonpayment unless the insurer gives notice at least 30 days before the lapse date, to the owner and to each designee, at the address the owner gave for lapse notices. (10113.72(c))
  • The notice goes by first-class US mail, within 30 days after a premium is due and unpaid. (10113.71(b), 10113.72(c))
  • A notice of pending lapse is not effective unless the insurer mails it at least 30 days before the policy ends for nonpayment, to the owner, the designee (for an individual policy), and any known assignee or other person with an interest in the individual policy. Notices to assignees may be electronic if the assignee agrees. (10113.71(b))
  • The section 10113.71 lapse-notice rule does not apply to nonrenewal. (10113.71(b))

Exam tip

Keep the three numbers apart: 60 days of grace, notice at least 30 days before lapse, and mailed within 30 days after the premium is unpaid.
