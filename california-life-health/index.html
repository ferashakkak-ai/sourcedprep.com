California life and health insurance exams

For the California Life Agent exam and the Accident and Health or Sickness Agent exam.

The California practice kit

The California Life and Health Retake Kit is in final checks. It goes on the shelf only when it passes them. To hear the day it does, ask for one email.

The two exams

The California Department of Insurance License Examination Objectives, effective January 1, 2026, set out two separate exams: Life Agent and Accident and Health or Sickness Agent. Each has 75 scored questions. The exams are given by PSI, which the objectives name as the exam provider.

Life Agent: 75 scored questions

  • General insurance: 22
  • Life insurance: 49
  • Riders: 4

Accident and Health or Sickness Agent: 75 scored questions

  • General insurance: 18
  • General concepts: 4
  • Medical expense: 45
  • Disability income: 4
  • Long-term care: 4

Source: California Department of Insurance, License Examination Objectives for the Life Agent and the Accident and Health or Sickness Agent examinations, effective January 1, 2026, read on November 12, 2026.

The course new applicants complete

New applicants complete a 12-hour course on ethics and the California Insurance Code, including one hour on insurance fraud (Cal. Ins. Code sec. 1749(a), effective January 1, 2026). Someone applying for more than one of those license types takes the course only once. No practice kit replaces it.

20 free California practice questions

Checked against the sources, read on November 12, 2026, and released on November 13, 2026.

Say your answer before you open it. In each answer, the line just above the source shows why a wrong choice can look right. These questions are a free set of their own: none of them is in the California kit.

  1. Priya states on her insurance application that she has no heart condition. To what time is that representation presumed to refer when its truth is judged?
    • A. To the day she first met the agent
    • B. To the date the underwriter finishes reviewing it
    • C. To the time the contract of insurance is completed
    • D. To the day she signed the application, before the contract is complete
    Show the answer

    C. To the time the contract of insurance is completed Insurance Code section 356 says the completion of the contract of insurance is the time to which a representation must be presumed to refer. The signing date feels natural, but the code ties a representation to the time the contract is completed.

    Source: Cal. Ins. Code sec. 356.

  2. Marcus has homes in California and in Nevada and holds a license under the Insurance Code chapter on licensing. How many states may he designate as his resident state?
    • A. Only one state, the one he designates as his resident state
    • B. Both states, if he lives in each of them part of the year
    • C. Any number of states in which he holds a home or a license
    • D. One state for life coverage and another for health coverage
    Show the answer

    A. Only one state, the one he designates as his resident state Under Insurance Code section 1638(b), a person licensed under that chapter may designate only one state as the person's resident state. Owning homes in two states does not allow two resident states: the licensee designates just one.

    Source: Cal. Ins. Code sec. 1638(b).

  3. An insurer's employee, intending to deceive the examiner lawfully appointed to examine the company, leaves a material fact about the insurer's business out of the insurer's report on purpose. How does the Insurance Code define this conduct?
    • A. As a permitted choice when the fact is later reported to the insurer's board
    • B. As a private matter between the insurer and its auditors
    • C. As a minor error unless the examiner suffers a financial loss
    • D. As an unfair and deceptive act or practice in the business of insurance
    Show the answer

    D. As an unfair and deceptive act or practice in the business of insurance Section 790.03(e) lists, among the unfair and deceptive acts or practices in the business of insurance, willfully omitting a true entry of a material fact with intent to deceive an examiner. Calling it an internal matter ignores that the section places it among the defined unfair and deceptive practices.

    Source: Cal. Ins. Code sec. 790.03(e).

  4. An insurer knowingly, and as a general business practice, holds back a clearly owed amount under one part of a policy, hoping the claimant will accept less on another part. How does the Insurance Code classify this?
    • A. As a fair bargaining tool, since the parts of a policy are separate contracts
    • B. As an unfair claims settlement practice the code defines
    • C. As a lawful practice when the claimant has a lawyer
    • D. As a lawful practice when the delay lasts less than a year
    Show the answer

    B. As an unfair claims settlement practice the code defines Section 790.03(h)(12) defines as an unfair claims settlement practice failing to settle claims promptly, where liability has become apparent, under one portion of the coverage to influence settlements under other portions. Treating each coverage part as a separate bargain does not excuse delay where liability is already apparent.

    Source: Cal. Ins. Code sec. 790.03(h)(12).

  5. An insurer and its agents are investigating a claim they suspect is fraudulent. Under Insurance Code section 1871.1, which records may they access?
    • A. The claimant's sealed court files, once fraud is suspected
    • B. Relevant public records the law already requires to be open
    • C. The claimant's private bank records, on the insurer's request
    • D. State agency files closed to the public, in fraud cases
    Show the answer

    B. Relevant public records the law already requires to be open Section 1871.1 gives insurers and their agents, while investigating suspected fraud claims, access to relevant public records that are required to be open for inspection under the Government Code, and it grants no access beyond existing law. Suspecting fraud does not open closed or private records: the section restates existing law and adds no new access.

    Source: Cal. Ins. Code sec. 1871.1.

  6. Ana is a producer. She helps a client write a statement she knows is false so it can support a claim for compensation as defined in Labor Code section 3207, but she does not file the claim herself. Under Insurance Code section 1871.4, how is Ana's help treated?
    • A. It is lawful, because the section reaches just the person who files the claim
    • B. It is lawful for a producer who receives no commission on the claim
    • C. It is unlawful only when the client pays her a fee for the help
    • D. It is unlawful to knowingly assist a person in an unlawful act
    Show the answer

    D. It is unlawful to knowingly assist a person in an unlawful act Section 1871.4(a)(2) makes it unlawful to present a knowingly false statement in support of a claim, and (a)(3) makes it unlawful to knowingly assist, abet, conspire with, or solicit a person in an unlawful act under the section. Not filing the claim yourself does not protect you when you knowingly assist the person who does.

    Source: Cal. Ins. Code sec. 1871.4(a)(3); Cal. Ins. Code 1871.4(a)(2).

  7. Harbor Light, a charity that meets the requirements of Revenue and Taxation Code section 214, wants to take out a life policy on Joe, a longtime donor. Joe consents to the policy. The charity gains no money from Joe staying alive. What does the Insurance Code allow?
    • A. The charity may effect the insurance, because Joe consents to it
    • B. The policy is void, because the charity has no insurable interest in Joe
    • C. The charity must also get written consent from Joe's children
    • D. The charity must name Joe's estate as the policy's beneficiary
    Show the answer

    A. The charity may effect the insurance, because Joe consents to it Section 10110.1(h) says that, notwithstanding the insurable interest rules in subdivisions (a), (f) and (g), a charitable organization meeting Revenue and Taxation Code section 214 or 23701d may effectuate life or disability insurance on an insured who consents. The usual insurable interest test in subdivision (g) does not void this policy: for a qualifying charity, the insured's consent is what the section asks for.

    Source: Cal. Ins. Code sec. 10110.1(h); Cal. Ins. Code 10110.1(g).

  8. Tomas is asked to sell an annuity product he has never studied, and he has not done the insurer's product training. What must be true before he solicits the sale under the Insurance Code?
    • A. He must have the customer sign a statement that no recommendation is wanted
    • B. He may solicit now and finish the insurer's training after the sale
    • C. He must know the product well enough and meet the insurer's training standards
    • D. He needs proof that he has sold annuities for at least one year
    Show the answer

    C. He must know the product well enough and meet the insurer's training standards Section 10509.915(a) says a producer shall not solicit the sale of an annuity product unless the producer has adequate knowledge of it to recommend it and complies with the insurer's standards for product training. A customer's statement or a track record does not replace adequate product knowledge and the insurer's training standards.

    Source: Cal. Ins. Code sec. 10509.915(a).

  9. Dev's agent is replacing his life policy with a new one from another insurer. The replacing insurer asks the existing insurer for a copy of the policy summaries or ledger statement. Within what time must the existing insurer furnish it?
    • A. Within 30 days after the new policy is delivered
    • B. Within five working days of receiving the request
    • C. Within 60 days, once the owner signs a release
    • D. Within one year after the replacement is done
    Show the answer

    B. Within five working days of receiving the request Under section 10509.6(b), the replacing insurer may ask the existing insurer for a copy of the summaries or ledger statement, and the copy is due within five working days of the receipt of the request. The time is short and runs from receipt of the request, not from delivery of the new policy.

    Source: Cal. Ins. Code sec. 10509.6(b).

  10. Rosa applies for individual life insurance, pays the full first premium and gets the insurer's receipt. She dies after her medical exam but before the policy is issued, and the insurer then approves the application under its usual standards. Setting aside any cap stated in the application, what must the insurer do?
    • A. The insurer refunds her premium and owes nothing else, because no policy was delivered
    • B. The insurer pays half of the amount applied for, because delivery had not happened
    • C. The insurer pays after the policy is delivered to a named beneficiary
    • D. The insurer pays as if the policy had been issued on the day she signed the application
    Show the answer

    D. The insurer pays as if the policy had been issued on the day she signed the application Section 10115 requires the insurer, in that situation, to pay the amount that would have been due as if the policy had been issued and delivered on the date the application was signed. Death before delivery does not end the insurer's duty when the full first premium was paid and the insurer later accepts the application under its usual standards.

    Source: Cal. Ins. Code sec. 10115.

  11. Ben has a qualified long-term care rider on his life insurance contract, and the rider's cost is charged against the contract's cash surrender value. Under federal tax law, may Ben take a medical expense deduction under section 213(a) for that payment?
    • A. The payment is deductible, since the long-term care rider is a qualified contract
    • B. Half of the amount is deductible, since the rider is part of a life contract
    • C. No deduction is allowed for a charge against the cash surrender value
    • D. The amount is deductible if the policy has no loans outstanding
    Show the answer

    C. No deduction is allowed for a charge against the cash surrender value Section 7702B(e)(2) denies any section 213(a) deduction for a payment for qualified long-term care coverage when the payment is made as a charge against the cash surrender value of a life insurance contract or the cash value of an annuity contract. The coverage being qualified does not make a payment charged against the cash value deductible.

    Source: 26 U.S.C. sec. 7702B(e)(2).

  12. A company's employees are covered by a valid group life policy. Another insurer wants to issue a replacement group life policy to the company. Which of these is one of the conditions in Insurance Code section 10203.9?
    • A. Ninety percent of all persons covered by the old policy join the new one
    • B. Covered employees of the company must pass a new group medical exam before the change
    • C. The new policy must charge a higher rate than the old one did
    • D. The new policy may leave out coverage for part-time workers and retirees
    Show the answer

    A. Ninety percent of all persons covered by the old policy join the new one Section 10203.9 allows the replacement only if the new policy gives the same benefits at the same or a lesser rate, offers them to all persons covered by the old policy, and 90 percent of those persons become insured under it. The conditions are about the same or lesser rate, offering coverage to all, and 90 percent joining, not new exams or higher rates.

    Source: Cal. Ins. Code sec. 10203.9.

  13. Marta's life policy has an accelerated death benefit and an outstanding policy loan. Paying the benefit cuts the policy's cash value pro rata. How much of the loan may the payment be applied toward repaying?
    • A. All of the outstanding loan on the policy, with the rest of the benefit paid to Marta
    • B. The unpaid interest, with the loan principal left untouched
    • C. None of it, because loans are never repaid from benefits
    • D. No more than a pro rata portion of any outstanding loan balance
    Show the answer

    D. No more than a pro rata portion of any outstanding loan balance Section 10295.7(d) says that when the payment causes a pro rata reduction in the cash value, it shall not be applied toward repaying more than a pro rata portion of any outstanding policy loans. The payment is not applied to the whole loan: only a pro rata portion may be applied.

    Source: Cal. Ins. Code sec. 10295.7(d).

  14. Wen buys an individual accident and health policy, issued for delivery in California, that is not a single-premium nonrenewable one. What must the policy have printed on it or attached to it about returning it after delivery?
    • A. A notice that she may return it for a refund if her first claim is denied
    • B. A notice that she may return it for a refund if she is not satisfied for any reason
    • C. A notice that she may return it, but the insurer keeps part of the premium
    • D. A notice giving her a return period of five days, counted from the day of delivery
    Show the answer

    B. A notice that she may return it for a refund if she is not satisfied for any reason Section 10276 requires a printed or attached notice that the purchaser may return the policy after delivery and have the premium refunded if, after examination, the purchaser is not satisfied for any reason. The right to return is not limited to a denied claim and does not leave part of the premium with the insurer.

    Source: Cal. Ins. Code sec. 10276.

  15. Nina's disability insurance policy covers perinatal services and was issued in 2026. A certified nurse-midwife provides a perinatal service to her. What must the policy contain?
    • A. A provision that pays the nurse-midwife directly for the service
    • B. A provision that pays the supervising physician for the service
    • C. A provision that excludes the certified nurse-midwife unless a doctor is present
    • D. A provision that pays Nina, who must then pay the midwife herself
    Show the answer

    A. A provision that pays the nurse-midwife directly for the service Section 10353(a) requires a policy offering perinatal coverage to provide direct reimbursement to certified nurse-midwives and nurse practitioners for perinatal services. Paying only the insured or a physician skips the direct reimbursement the section requires.

    Source: Cal. Ins. Code sec. 10353(a).

  16. A disability insurance policy issued in California pays part of the cost of a sterilization procedure. The insurer adds an exclusion that applies based on the reason the covered person asks for the procedure. How does the Insurance Code treat that exclusion?
    • A. The exclusion is valid if the policy states it in bold type
    • B. The exclusion is valid when the reason given is personal choice
    • C. The exclusion is void and of no effect for that reason
    • D. The exclusion is valid if the commissioner reviews the form first
    Show the answer

    C. The exclusion is void and of no effect for that reason Section 10120 says any exclusion, reduction, or limitation on such a benefit based on the covered person's reasons for requesting sterilization is void and of no effect. Bold type or form review cannot rescue an exclusion the section declares void.

    Source: Cal. Ins. Code sec. 10120.

  17. A Medicare supplement policy says it will pay benefits based on what is "usual and customary." How does the Insurance Code treat that wording?
    • A. The wording is required so supplement benefits match what Medicare allows
    • B. It is allowed if the policy explains how the amount is found
    • C. It is allowed in group certificates but not in individual policies
    • D. A policy of that kind shall not pay benefits on that standard
    Show the answer

    D. A policy of that kind shall not pay benefits on that standard Section 10192.17(c) says Medicare supplement policies or certificates shall not provide for payment of benefits based on standards described as usual and customary, reasonable and customary, or words of similar import. Explaining the method does not make the standard allowed: the section bars it, and group certificates are covered too.

    Source: Cal. Ins. Code sec. 10192.17(c).

  18. Carl has a Medicare supplement policy and paid premium in advance. He asks the issuer to end his coverage. What must the issuer do?
    • A. Keep the unearned premium as a cancellation charge and end coverage
    • B. Refund any unearned premium and end the coverage when he asks
    • C. End the coverage but credit the unearned premium to a future policy
    • D. Continue coverage to the end of the period he paid for, with no refund
    Show the answer

    B. Refund any unearned premium and end the coverage when he asks Section 10192.12(g) says an issuer shall refund any unearned premium the insured paid in advance and shall terminate coverage upon the insured's request. The issuer cannot keep or reroute unearned premium or delay ending the coverage once the insured asks.

    Source: Cal. Ins. Code sec. 10192.12(g).

  19. Claudia gives her insurer notice of a claim under her disability policy. The insurer does not furnish claim forms within 15 days. What does the required claim forms provision say about her proof of loss?
    • A. She is treated as complying once she sends written proof of the loss within the policy's time limit
    • B. The time limit for her proof of loss is paused until the insurer finally sends her the forms
    • C. Her claim is lost, because the insurer's own forms are what count as her proof of loss, not hers
    • D. She must ask a court to excuse the missing forms before she is allowed to file any proof
    Show the answer

    A. She is treated as complying once she sends written proof of the loss within the policy's time limit Section 10350.6 says that if the forms are not furnished within 15 days of notice, the claimant is deemed to have complied by submitting written proof of the occurrence, character and extent of the loss within the time fixed in the policy. Missing forms do not pause the deadline or cost her the claim: written proof within the policy's time limit is enough.

    Source: Cal. Ins. Code sec. 10350.6.

  20. A long-term care insurer is about to pay benefits for care covered by the policy. Under California law, what may the insurer first obtain before paying?
    • A. A written promise from the insured to repay benefits if the care proves unneeded
    • B. A signed statement from the insured's relatives agreeing the care is needed
    • C. An independent written declaration that the services are necessary
    • D. A written approval of the facility's charges from its owner or manager
    Show the answer

    C. An independent written declaration that the services are necessary Section 10233 lets the insurer obtain a written declaration by a physician, independent needs assessment agency, or other independent source suitable to the insurer that services are necessary. Relatives or the facility owner are not independent sources of judgment about necessity.

    Source: Cal. Ins. Code sec. 10233.
