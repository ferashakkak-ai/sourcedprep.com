Replacing life insurance or an annuity in Texas: what the agent must do

Last checked 13 November 2026.

Chapter 1114 of the Texas Insurance Code sets rules for replacing an existing life insurance policy or annuity. Their purpose is to protect buyers in replacement and financed-purchase transactions. (1114.001(1), (2))

What counts as a replacement

A replacement is buying a new policy or contract when the agent or insurer knows, or should know, that as a result an existing policy or contract has been or will be:

  • lapsed, forfeited, surrendered (in full or in part), assigned to the replacing insurer or otherwise ended;
  • converted to reduced paid-up or extended term insurance, or reduced in value through its nonforfeiture benefits or other values;
  • changed to cut its benefits, or the term for which it would stay in force or pay benefits;
  • reissued with less cash value; or
  • used in a financed purchase. (1114.002(8))

The agent's duties

  1. Ask about existing coverage. With the application, the agent gives the insurer a statement, which the applicant and the agent both sign, on whether the applicant already holds any policy or contract. (1114.051(a))
  2. If there is none, the replacement duties end there. (1114.051(b))
  3. If there is existing coverage, the agent presents and reads a replacement notice to the applicant, no later than when the application is taken. (1114.051(c)) The notice is on a form the commissioner adopts or approves (an adopted form trimmed only of references that do not apply needs no separate approval). The applicant and the agent both sign it, confirming it was read aloud or that the applicant chose not to have it read. The agent leaves the notice with the applicant. It lists each policy or annuity to be replaced. (1114.051(d), (e))
  4. Leave the sales material. The agent leaves the applicant the original or a copy of all sales material used. (1114.051(f))
  5. Send the documents to the insurer. The agent gives the insurer a copy of each required document, a statement naming any insurer-approved sales material used, and copies of any individualized sales material, including illustrations. An insurer that allows only its own approved sales material may use a different procedure instead. (1114.051(g), 1114.053(g))

The 30-day right to return

In a replacement, the replacing insurer must tell the owner of the right to return the new policy or contract within 30 days of delivery for an unconditional full refund of premiums, including policy fees and charges. For variable or market value adjusted contracts, the refund is the cash surrender value plus the fees and charges deducted. (1114.053(e))
