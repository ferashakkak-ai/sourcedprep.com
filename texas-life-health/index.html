Texas life and health insurance exam: free practice questions

For the Texas Life, Accident and Health agent exam. 20 free questions, each with the answer, the reason, and the section of the Texas Insurance Code or the government page it rests on.

Say your answer before you open it. The line in italics shows why a wrong choice can look right. These questions are a free set of their own: none of them is in the Retake Kit.

Study article: The Texas life and health exams: time, fee, passing score and question counts

  1. A client's permanent life policy ties its cash value growth to sub-accounts the client picks, which hold stocks, bonds, or both. What does TDI say about that cash value?
    • A. It stays fixed at the figure shown when the policy was first issued
    • B. It can only grow, because the insurer absorbs every investment loss
    • C. It may go up or down, depending on how the sub-accounts perform
    • D. It matches the total premiums paid, whatever the sub-accounts earn
    Show the answer

    C. It may go up or down, depending on how the sub-accounts perform TDI explains that when cash value is tied to sub-accounts invested in stocks, bonds, or both, the value can rise or fall with how those sub-accounts do. The option saying the cash value can only grow ignores the page's point that the value depends on sub-account performance and could go down.

    Source: Texas Department of Insurance, Life insurance guide.

  2. A term policyholder worries that worsening health might make new coverage hard to get later. What does TDI say a convertibility feature lets the policyholder do?
    • A. Exchange the term policy for a permanent one with no medical exam or health questions
    • B. Exchange the term policy for a permanent one only after passing a fresh medical exam
    • C. Keep the term policy and add permanent coverage with no rise in premium
    • D. Extend the same term policy for another term whatever the person's health
    Show the answer

    A. Exchange the term policy for a permanent one with no medical exam or health questions TDI describes convertibility as swapping a term policy for a permanent policy without a medical exam or health questions. It adds that converting raises premiums and that companies usually allow it only for a time. Extending the same policy for more terms is renewability, which TDI treats as a separate feature; convertibility moves the owner into permanent coverage and raises the premium.

    Source: Texas Department of Insurance, Life insurance guide.

  3. A buyer takes delivery of a fixed annuity contract and soon changes her mind. What must the contract allow, under Texas law?
    • A. Rescission for at least 10 days, with the insurer keeping any contract fees
    • B. Rescission for at least 20 days, but only if the insurer agrees to the refund
    • C. Rescission only through a surrender, with the insurer keeping any charges
    • D. Rescission for at least 20 days, refunding premiums and any contract fees
    Show the answer

    D. Rescission for at least 20 days, refunding premiums and any contract fees A fixed annuity contract must give the purchaser at least 20 days after delivery to rescind and get back premiums paid, including contract fees or charges, with no conditions. A refund that lets the insurer keep fees falls short of the statute, which covers premiums and any contract fees or charges.

    Source: Tex. Ins. Code sec. 1116.002(a).

  4. A policy owner asks what an accidental death rider adds to a life policy. According to TDI, what does the rider do?
    • A. It adds term coverage on top of a permanent policy at no added cost
    • B. It provides an extra payment if the insured dies because of an accident
    • C. It lets the owner buy more coverage later whatever the insured's health
    • D. It pays a larger amount at death whatever the cause of death may be
    Show the answer

    B. It provides an extra payment if the insured dies because of an accident TDI says an accidental death rider provides an additional payment when the insured dies because of an accident, and that some restrictions apply. The rider's extra payment depends on the death being caused by an accident, so it does not pay more for every cause of death.

    Source: Texas Department of Insurance, Life insurance guide.

  5. A life policy has been in force for two years from its issue date, and the insured is still living. For which listed reason may the insurer still contest the policy?
    • A. Nonpayment of the premiums that were due under the policy
    • B. A health condition the applicant left off the application
    • C. A change in the insured's job after the policy was issued
    • D. A mistake in the beneficiary's name found after two years
    Show the answer

    A. Nonpayment of the premiums that were due under the policy Apart from the optional subsection (b) clause on wartime naval and military service conditions, a life policy in force two years from issue during the insured's lifetime must be incontestable except for nonpayment of premiums. Of the listed reasons, only nonpayment remains. A condition left off the application is not one of the statute's grounds; after two years only nonpayment of premiums remains, plus any wartime military service clause the policy includes.

    Source: Tex. Ins. Code sec. 1101.006(a) and (b).

  6. A producer is submitting a new life policy application. What must go to the insurer along with it?
    • A. A statement signed by the agent alone about the applicant's health
    • B. A copy of the applicant's latest tax return, signed by the agent
    • C. A signed statement from the applicant and agent on existing policies
    • D. A statement signed by the underwriter about the applicant's age
    Show the answer

    C. A signed statement from the applicant and agent on existing policies The agent who initiates the application must submit a statement signed by both the applicant and the agent on whether the applicant has existing policies or contracts. A statement signed by the agent alone fails the rule, which requires both the applicant and the agent to sign.

    Source: Tex. Ins. Code sec. 1114.051(a).

  7. A small business with a few staff members wants a group life policy for its employees. What enrollment floor does Texas set on the issue date?
    • A. At least five employees
    • B. At least ten employees
    • C. At least twenty-five employees
    • D. At least two employees
    Show the answer

    D. At least two employees The statute says the policy must cover at least two employees on the date it is issued. Five is the tempting round figure, but the statute sets the minimum at two employees.

    Source: Tex. Ins. Code sec. 1131.204(a).

  8. A shop owner with two employees asks what business disability insurance might pay for, according to TDI. Which choice fits?
    • A. Only the owner's personal medical bills after a covered illness or injury
    • B. Possibly premises costs, utilities, staff pay and benefits, and property taxes
    • C. Repairs to the shop after a fire or storm, but none of its running costs
    • D. Possibly the owner's lost income, though never any of the business expenses
    Show the answer

    B. Possibly premises costs, utilities, staff pay and benefits, and property taxes TDI says business disability insurance potentially pays business costs such as costs of the premises, utilities, staff pay and benefits, and property taxes. Limiting it to the owner's own income ignores the page, which lists business expenses the coverage may pay.

    Source: Texas Department of Insurance, disability insurance tip.

  9. A preferred provider is paid on a discounted fee basis. What must the provider's contract say the insured may be billed?
    • A. The full charge, less whatever the insurer paid the provider
    • B. The full charge, if the insurer is slow to pay the claim
    • C. The discounted fee, plus any added amount the provider sets
    • D. Only the discounted fee that applies, and not the full charge
    Show the answer

    D. Only the discounted fee that applies, and not the full charge The preferred provider contract must have the provider agree that, when paid on a discounted fee basis, the insured is billed the discounted fee only. Billing the full charge, even after taking off what the insurer paid, is still billing on the full charge, which the provision rules out; the insured may be billed only on the discounted fee.

    Source: Tex. Ins. Code sec. 1301.060.

  10. A buyer comparing long-term care policies wants a lower premium. What does TDI say a longer elimination period does?
    • A. It can lower the premium, but the buyer bears costs personally for longer
    • B. It raises the premium, because the insurer waits longer before it pays
    • C. It shortens the time the buyer pays personally, at a higher premium
    • D. It leaves the premium alone and only changes the benefit amount
    Show the answer

    A. It can lower the premium, but the buyer bears costs personally for longer TDI says a longer elimination period can lower the premium, at the price of the buyer paying out of pocket for a longer time before benefits start. The idea that a longer wait raises the premium reverses TDI's point, which links a longer wait to a lower premium.

    Source: Texas Department of Insurance, Long-term care insurance guide.

  11. An insurer describes a policy as guaranteed renewable. How does Texas law treat that kind of insurance policy?
    • A. As a one-year policy that ends unless the insurer chooses to renew it
    • B. As a continuous policy that the insurer may cancel at any time it wishes
    • C. As a continuous policy, subject only to its terms, such as premium payment
    • D. As a temporary policy that ends once the first claim has been paid out
    Show the answer

    C. As a continuous policy, subject only to its terms, such as premium payment A guaranteed renewable policy is treated as a continuous policy, subject only to its terms and conditions, including paying the premium. A policy the insurer may cancel at will is not continuous; the statute limits termination to the policy's own terms and conditions.

    Source: Tex. Ins. Code sec. 1202.001(a).

  12. A buyer asks whether an individual accident and health policy may include a provision letting the insurer defend a claim on a preexisting condition. What is the general rule in Section 1201.151?
    • A. Such a provision is permitted in every policy, with no limit at all
    • B. It is barred, except as that subchapter of the Insurance Code provides
    • C. Such a provision is required in every individual policy that is issued
    • D. Whether to include one is left wholly to each insurer to decide
    Show the answer

    B. It is barred, except as that subchapter of the Insurance Code provides Section 1201.151 bars a provision that permits a defense based on a preexisting condition, except as the subchapter itself provides. Leaving it to each insurer ignores the statute, which bars the provision except where the subchapter itself allows it.

    Source: Tex. Ins. Code sec. 1201.151.

  13. A private employer in Texas chooses to cancel its workers' compensation policy. From what point is it a non-subscriber, per the Division of Workers' Compensation?
    • A. From the day that its last open claim is finally closed
    • B. From the day thirty days after the policy has ended
    • C. From the day that the policy itself comes to an end
    • D. From the first day of the following calendar year
    Show the answer

    C. From the day that the policy itself comes to an end The Division says an employer that decides to end its coverage becomes a non-subscriber on the date the policy ends. Waiting for claims to close or for a later date adds steps the page does not mention; the change happens when the policy ends.

    Source: Texas Division of Workers' Compensation, non-subscriber page.

  14. After an applicant signs a written application for an individual accident and health policy, an agent wants to change one answer. What must the agent have first?
    • A. Written consent from the applicant to make the change
    • B. A verbal go-ahead from the insurer's own underwriter
    • C. A written order from the commissioner of insurance
    • D. The signature of a witness on the changed answer
    Show the answer

    A. Written consent from the applicant to make the change No person may alter a written application for an individual accident and health policy without the applicant's written consent. Permission from the insurer or a witness does not meet the rule; the consent has to be the applicant's and in writing.

    Source: Tex. Ins. Code sec. 1201.271(a).

  15. A student asks how a person becomes the Texas commissioner of insurance. Which description is correct?
    • A. Voters elect the commissioner at a statewide general election
    • B. The senate appoints, with the consent of the governor
    • C. The attorney general appoints, with the nod of the legislature
    • D. The governor appoints, with the advice and consent of the senate
    Show the answer

    D. The governor appoints, with the advice and consent of the senate Section 31.022(a) says the governor appoints the commissioner with the advice and consent of the senate, for a two-year term. Reversing the roles gives the senate the appointing power, but the statute puts the governor first and the senate in the advice and consent role.

    Source: Tex. Ins. Code sec. 31.022(a).

  16. An agent licensed in Texas relocates to a different state. Within what time must the agent report the new address and proof of authority there to the department?
    • A. Not later than the 10th day after moving
    • B. Not later than the 30th day after moving
    • C. Not later than the 60th day after moving
    • D. Not later than the 90th day after moving
    Show the answer

    B. Not later than the 30th day after moving The agent must file the new address and proof of authorization in the new state of residence not later than the 30th day after moving. Sixty days seems a generous window, but the statute sets the limit at the 30th day after the move.

    Source: Tex. Ins. Code sec. 4003.009(a).

  17. A Texas agent wants to pay part of a commission to a person who holds no Texas agent license, for services that person performed as an agent. What does Texas law say?
    • A. It is allowed, if the agent first tells the insurer in writing
    • B. It is allowed, if the amount paid stays below a set dollar limit
    • C. It is prohibited, since the person holds no license to act as an agent
    • D. It is prohibited only when the payment goes straight to that person
    Show the answer

    C. It is prohibited, since the person holds no license to act as an agent An insurer or agent may not pay, directly or indirectly, or accept a commission or other valuable consideration for agent services unless the person holds a license to act as an agent in Texas. Telling the insurer in writing does not cure the problem; the statute turns on whether the person holds an agent license.

    Source: Tex. Ins. Code sec. 4005.053(a).

  18. An agent is about to recommend an annuity to a consumer. Which standard does Texas law set for that recommendation?
    • A. Act in the consumer's best interest, not putting the agent's or insurer's interest first
    • B. Recommend the annuity that the insurer's own financial interest favors the most
    • C. Recommend any annuity so long as the consumer does not object to the sale being made
    • D. Recommend the annuity that pays the highest commission, if the consumer can afford it
    Show the answer

    A. Act in the consumer's best interest, not putting the agent's or insurer's interest first The agent must act in the consumer's best interest under the circumstances known when the recommendation is made, without putting the agent's or insurer's financial interest ahead of the consumer's. A recommendation the consumer merely does not object to can still put the agent's or insurer's interest first, which the statute forbids.

    Source: Tex. Ins. Code sec. 1115.051(a).

  19. An insured's application for an individual accident and health policy had a false statement, and the insurer denies a claim because of it. When does the falsity bar recovery?
    • A. Whenever the statement is false, however small the false point may be
    • B. Only if it materially affected the insurer's acceptance of the risk
    • C. Only if the insured knew it was false at the moment of signing
    • D. Only if the statement concerned the applicant's age or occupation
    Show the answer

    B. Only if it materially affected the insurer's acceptance of the risk A false statement does not bar recovery unless it materially affected the acceptance of the risk or the hazard the insurer assumed. Treating every false answer as a bar ignores the materiality test the statute sets.

    Source: Tex. Ins. Code sec. 1201.272.

  20. An HMO wishes to end an enrollee's coverage. On which grounds may it cancel or decline to renew that coverage?
    • A. Heavy use of services, or a rise in the organization's own costs
    • B. A change of the enrollee's primary care physician, or a complaint
    • C. Any reason named in the organization's own contract, with notice given
    • D. Nonpayment of charges, or another reason set by commissioner rules
    Show the answer

    D. Nonpayment of charges, or another reason set by commissioner rules An HMO may cancel or refuse to renew an enrollee's coverage only for failure to pay the charges or another reason prescribed by commissioner rules. A reason written only into the HMO's own contract is not enough; the other ground must be one prescribed by the commissioner's rules.

    Source: Tex. Ins. Code sec. 843.208.
